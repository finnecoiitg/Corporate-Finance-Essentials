About the course

This two-week intensive course on Corporate Finance is designed to build a strong foundation in financial decision-making with a clear focus on real-world application. The course covers key concepts such as value of money, risk and return, capital budgeting, cost of capital, financing decisions, financial statements, and essential financial metrics used in practice.

  • Structured 12-day learning path
  • Curated readings and references
  • Two quizzes to test your understanding
  • Designed by FEC IIT Guwahati

Resources

12-day structured learning plan

01

Foundations of Finance & Financial Markets

Finance, Saving/Investing, Liquidity, Financial Market & Types

02

Capital Markets Overview

A guide to long-term financing, equity, debt, and trading.

 03

Market Efficiency & EMH

Understand how financial markets reflect information and why consistently beating the market is difficult. Explore the three forms of EMH, real-world criticisms, and how mispricing disappears quickly in efficient markets.

04

Investment Funds & Portfolio Strategies

Explore how pooled investment vehicles help individuals and institutions build diversified, goal-oriented portfolios. This day focuses on understanding mutual funds, ETFs, pension funds, alternative investment funds (AIFs), portfolio management services (PMS), and index funds.

 05

Banking system

This day we dive deep into types of banking facilities, from commercial to retail to central national banking agencies, followed by the types of accounts and a guide on how to choose one.

 06

NBFCs and Microlending

Explains NBFCs and microlending, their role in financial inclusion, differences from banks, types, services, risks, regulation by RBI, and their importance in expanding credit access.

 07

Risk, Return & Portfolio Risk Management

Learn how risk and return are connected and how investors balance both to build efficient portfolios. This session covers return calculation, volatility and standard deviation, the Efficient Frontier, and the power of diversification. It also introduces key risk measures like VaR and shortfall risk, along with practical strategies such as asset allocation, hedging, and emphasizing that risk cannot be eliminated, only managed effectively.

 08

CAPM, Market Risk & Cost of Capital

This session explains how risk is priced in financial markets using the Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM). Learn how the risk-free rate and risk premium determine expected returns, why only systematic risk is rewarded, and how beta measures market sensitivity. Explore the Security Market Line (SML) and Capital Market Line (CML) to understand asset and portfolio pricing, and connect these concepts to corporate finance through WACC—showing how market risk translates into valuation, capital budgeting, and investment decisions.

 09

Financial metrics

Financial ratios like Beta, Sharpe, Sortino, and Jensen's Alpha are critical tools for evaluating investment performance by adjusting returns for risk rather than looking at profit alone. Beta measures a security's volatility relative to the overall market (systematic risk), while Jensen's Alpha quantifies the "abnormal" return generated by a manager's skill above a theoretical benchmark.
